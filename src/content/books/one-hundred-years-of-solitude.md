García Márquez's prose is hypnotic. The Buendía family's saga unfolds with a dreamlike quality that blurs the line between reality and fantasy. The cyclical nature of time in Macondo is both haunting and beautiful—each generation seems to repeat the mistakes of the last, yet each story feels entirely new.

What strikes me most is how the author uses magical realism not as mere fantasy, but as a lens to examine Colombia's history and the human condition. The supernatural elements feel more real than reality itself.
